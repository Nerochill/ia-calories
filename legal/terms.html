6AI features and their limits

How the AI features work

Meal analysis. When you scan a meal, your photo is uploaded to our image-storage provider. A resized copy is then sent to our AI provider, OpenAI, which identifies the foods it can see. For each food it estimates a portion, its weight in grams, calories, protein, carbohydrates and fat, along with a name for the meal. Names are returned in the language you've selected.

Daily targets. When you finish onboarding, your answers (sex, age, height, weight, activity level, goal and diet preference) are sent to OpenAI, which generates daily calorie and macronutrient targets. We then apply automatic safeguards. Calorie targets are kept at or above 1,200 kcal for women and 1,500 kcal for men, and at or below 6,000 kcal. Macronutrients are rescaled when they don't add up to the calorie target.

Limits you should know about

  • Estimates can be wrong. AI can misidentify foods, misjudge portion sizes, and miss ingredients it can't see, such as oils, sauces, sugar or fillings. The accuracy of any result is not guaranteed.
  • Photos without food are rejected. If the AI decides a photo doesn't show food or drink, the scan fails and nothing is logged.
  • Analysis can fail. Scans may fail or be delayed because of technical problems with us or our providers. The app shows these scans as failed, and you can take another photo.
  • Results may vary. The same or similar photos can produce different results.

You are responsible for deciding whether and how to rely on any AI output. Use your own judgment, and check product labels or a professional when accuracy matters to you.
